===========================================================================
SPENDING THE FULL 2-MINUTE BUDGET
===========================================================================

Recording is nearly free, so the real budget is not the ~6 s gap before the next
transmission but most of the next receive period: roughly 100 s x 8 cores, about
200x what the decoder currently uses.  The question is what that buys.

JOINT CHANNEL-AND-DATA SCORING  (tools/glrt.c.inc, src/wsprd-glrt)

The idea: hand the decoder a long list of candidate codewords and pick between
them with a statistic it did not optimise.  For each candidate, estimate the
channel FROM that candidate and measure how coherently the data lines up.  The
right codeword gives a clean channel estimate; a wrong one estimates the channel
partly from noise.  Scoring one candidate costs ~500 operations once the
per-tone correlations exist, so a list of thousands is affordable.

Measured over 4141 coherent trials at -31 to -34 dB, logging the score of the
TRUE codeword and of the one OSD actually returned:

  OSD returned the truth        626 trials   score p5=13.8  p50=16.0
  OSD returned something else  3515 trials   score p5= 6.3  p50= 8.8  max=17.4
  the true codeword outscores OSD's wrong one in only 5.7% of those

REFUTED, and the reason matters.  Splitting the wrong-codeword cases by how
wrong they are:

  60+ symbols wrong   n=2919   true scores 2.9,  OSD's noise fit scores 8.5
  40..59 wrong        n= 591   true scores 12.2, OSD's       scores 12.7

The first group is the decoder chewing on noise at parameters where no signal
exists; the true codeword correctly scores nothing there.  The second group is
the killer: at the right parameters, a codeword ~50 symbols from the truth fits
the noisy data slightly BETTER than the truth does.  That is what operating at
the code's limit without a CRC means, and no amount of search fixes it.

As an acceptance test the ROC has no usable operating point: threshold 17 keeps
only 22% of correct OSD finds and still admits 1 in 3515 wrong ones, which is
about one bad spot per 240 files.  Worse in deployment, because taking the best
score over a large trial grid pushes the proposed value into the extreme tail.

CONCLUSION.  Extra compute buys more SEARCH, and search only helps where
acceptance is already safe.  For a callsign already in the hash table that is
true, and more search does help.  For a new callsign the binding constraint is
WSPR's 50-bit message with no CRC, not CPU.  The 0.90 dB that OSD gives known
stations cannot be extended to unknown ones by any single-decode statistic
tested here.

WHAT DOES SCALE WITH COMPUTE
  - the blanker sweep, finer and wider
  - more demodulator trials (these use the normal, safe acceptance)
  - more subtraction passes on crowded bands
  - deeper OSD for known callsigns, where the hash gate is a strong check

CPU OPTIMISATIONS (bit-identical output, verified against the branch tip at
-P 1, -P 8 and -P 16 with the full stack)

  stage                     before    after
  coarse DT/freq/drift      0.32 s    0.17 s     square roots taken once per bin
  sync refinement           0.67 s    0.36 s     tone tables built once per symbol
  whole decode, 1 worker    7.82 s    7.21 s     -7.8%
  whole decode, 8 workers   2.20 s    2.09 s     -5.0%

The coarse search gained less than the call count suggested because gcc at -O3
was already vectorising those square roots.  The sync refinement halving is as
predicted: the tone tables are only rebuilt per symbol when drift is non-zero,
which is passes one and two.

A-PRIORI DETECTION OF KNOWN STATIONS  (src/wsprd-ap, research build)

With a realistic prior -- frequency to +/-0.1 Hz and DT to +/-0.15 s, both from
the last time the station was decoded -- and 30 known stations tested, each only
near its own frequency:

  threshold 14: one false spot per 26 hours per band, 63.6% detection at -34 dB
  50% detection lands near -34.3 dB, against a -32.35 dB decode threshold

so about 2 dB, on top of the 1.4 dB already committed.  Longer coherent
integration adds little: 55 s of smoothing instead of 18 s moved -34 detection
from 63.6% to 70.0%.

CAVEATS, both measured rather than assumed:
  - stable paths only.  On Rayleigh fading with 0.1 Hz spread the detector gives
    23% at -30 dB against a -29.85 dB decode threshold, i.e. nothing.  Same
    constraint as the coherent demodulator: the channel has to hold still.
  - known stations only.  It does nothing for new DX, which is what people chase.
  - the gain is set by how many hypotheses are searched, so it depends on
    testing only recently-active stations rather than everything ever heard.

THE FANO BUDGET, REVISITED (2026-08-29, results/cfano/NOTES.md)
Can acceptance-side gating buy back a bigger -C?  No, twice: false accepts and
marginal real decodes at -C 10000/50000 are statistically the same population
(best zero-false cut keeps 2.3% of the gain, 1296 real vs 94 false events,
9600 paired trials), and hash-gated escalation (-E 50000) on top of full Max
adds 1 decode in 1200 known-station trials because OSD plus recall already
saturate the gated deep search.  Also new: -C 50000 LOSES real decodes at
easier SNRs through wrong-accept subtraction damage.  -C 500 confirmed clean
at scale: 0 false in 9600 trials.  -C 500 stays.
